# The questions Nathan asked

Working notes for the four how-to posts. This is a paraphrased, anonymised list of every question Nathan asked on one advisory call with the founder of a seed-stage AI startup. The call ran about ninety minutes.

## How to read this

- The company is "a seed-stage AI startup" and the person is "the founder". No names, no customers, no competitors, no places, no product details.
- Every question is paraphrased. None of these are verbatim quotes.
- The questions are grouped by the post they feed. A few fit two posts, and those are listed once under the best fit with a cross-reference.
- "Asked" means Nathan put it to the founder and waited for an answer. "Posed" means he raised it as part of explaining the method, as a question the founder should be able to answer each week.
- The raw transcripts and notes are private and are not in this repo.

Near the end of the call Nathan told the founder that he had just walked them through more or less what a VC would ask in due diligence. That is why the first group is the longest.

## 1. How to survive VC due diligence meetings

Post: `/blog/how-to-survive-vc-due-diligence-meetings`

### The vision

1. (Asked) If someone waved a magic wand and everything went right for the company, what does the big picture look like?
2. (Asked) Is there a revenue number attached to that picture, and over what period?
3. (Asked) In that picture, what is the company actually doing, and who are its customers?

### The customers

4. (Asked) Can you list your top five or ten target customers off the top of your head?
5. (Asked) Are those targets all one type of buyer, or are there neighbouring groups that buy for the same reason?
6. (Asked, then dropped mid-sentence) What is the smallest customer you could sensibly serve? (Picked up again later as question 21.)

### The founder and the team

7. (Asked) Looking back across your career, which companies, and which people at those companies, did you most enjoy working with? (Also feeds positioning.)
8. (Asked) At the company I assumed you came from, who were your counterparts on the customer side? (The founder had not worked there, which led to the next question.)
9. (Asked) Where did you work when you were doing the hands-on operational work?
10. (Asked) Who were that firm's clients, and what did the firm do for them?
11. (Asked) At those clients, who did you deal with day to day? Were you close to the work itself, or was it more hands-off?
12. (Asked) Who do you know? What kinds of firms and people are in your network, and who do you end up talking to at a conference, a founders' meetup, or with friends in the industry?

### The competition

13. (Asked) Who else does this today? Who does exactly this?
14. (Asked) Does the best-known incumbent do it?
15. (Asked) Which firms do you know of that are building it themselves instead of buying?
16. (Asked) Is one particular firm I have in mind among them?

## 2. How to position a startup

Post: `/blog/how-to-position-a-startup`

### Where the pull is

17. (Asked) Where is your message getting the most traction?
18. (Asked) Where are the conversations easiest? Not easy, but where does it not feel like pushing a rock up a hill?

### What the product is, in plain words

19. (Asked) When you say that one part of the product is your strongest, what is that part in plain words? Is it the thing I think it is?
20. (Asked) How do you sell the product today? Say I am a named prospect and I tell you I love it. What do I get, and where should we start?
21. (Asked) Now say I have never seen it. I take your word for it and I am buying. What do I get on day one, and how do I use it?

### Who it is for

22. (Asked) At what size does doing it yourself stop working? Is it a headcount, a volume of work, a number of outside parties they deal with? Where does a buyer start to need or want this?
23. (Posed) Can a buyer answer "do you need this, yes or no?" as soon as they hear what you do?

### Proof

24. (Posed) Is the first customer the only reason anyone would take the call? Nathan's view was no. Experience, know-how and the product itself count as proof too.

## 3. How to prioritize as a startup founder

Post: `/blog/how-to-prioritize-as-a-startup-founder`

### Where to start

25. (Asked) You have talked to several advisors already. Where do you feel the greatest need?
26. (Posed, echoing the founder) Where should you start?
27. (Posed) What is the tip of the spear? What does the rest of the team think it is?

### The weekly loop

28. (Posed, for the Monday meeting) What are the priorities for this week?
29. (Posed, for the Monday meeting) Who is going to focus on what?
30. (Posed, for the review later in the week) What worked?
31. (Posed, for the review later in the week) What did not work?
32. (Posed, for the review later in the week) Did your numbers go up or not, and why?

### What the founder asked back

These are the founder's questions, not Nathan's. They are here because the prioritization post answers them.

- With limited time, a small team and limited money, how do you get anything done?
- With this many good ideas on the table, how do you decide where to start?
- How many people do we need working on the same problem before it stops being productive?

## 4. Data-driven decision making

Post: `/blog/data-driven-decision-making-for-startups`

### The number

33. (Asked) Do you have a North Star metric? Which number would you most like to see change?
34. (Asked) What is the most important number to you? What comes to mind first? It can be more than one, and it can be any kind of number.
35. (Posed, about his own company) Which numbers are we looking at? Which metrics matter? Nathan said he was still asking this three or four years into his own company, and that pricing was the hardest part.

### The weekly read

36. (Posed) What is working and what is not?
37. (Posed) How are the metrics changing from week to week?
38. (Posed) How does that feed into sales?
39. (Posed) Are opportunities moving through the pipeline?
40. (Posed) Are more leads coming in at the top of the funnel?

### The experiments

41. (Posed, about landing pages with a form) What are people coming here for? Is it the core capability, the developer tools, or the done-for-you service?

## Not included

- Scheduling questions about when to begin.
- Anything about fees or the commercial relationship.
- Clarifying questions that only repeat a name.
