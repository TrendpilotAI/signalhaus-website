# Toolkit copy angles

Web-copy angles drawn from Nathan's Data-Driven Decision-Making Toolkit and its worked examples. These are raw material for the landing page, the four posts and the lead forms.

Canonical URL, used for every backlink on the site:
https://gamma.app/docs/Data-Driven-Decision-Making-Toolkit-From-Analytics-to-Visualiza-smz2m6k9t7u666d

## Ground rules for using this

- Link to the toolkit, do not reproduce it. Summaries on the site are in our own words.
- The worked examples in the toolkit belong to named participants. On the site they are anonymised to a business type ("a manufacturer", "a health-tech founder") and carry no names, budgets or results.
- The toolkit quotes a few figures as rules of thumb (a one-hour setup, a 15-minute weekly review, tests under two weeks and under $500, five metrics at most). These are the toolkit's own guidelines, so the site attributes them to the toolkit and never presents them as client results.
- Logos and affiliations shown inside the toolkit deck are not repeated on the site.

## The core ideas, in one line each

| Toolkit idea | Plain version for the site |
|---|---|
| North Star Lite metric | Pick the smallest number that proves you learned something this week. |
| Progress in a Box | One sheet, one metric, one short review. About an hour to set up. |
| Three-field tracker | Date, the number, one line on why it moved. |
| Insight Loop | After each result, three questions. Why did this happen? What will we try next? What is in the way? |
| KIS guardrails | One primary goal. Five metrics at most. One owner per metric. |
| Decision threshold | Decide in advance how much data is enough to act. |
| Goal, then metric, then picture | No goal, no metric. No metric, no chart. |
| Pre-mortem question | If this number moves and the goal does not, why would that be? |
| Weekly alignment tick | Green if the number is moving the right way, amber if flat, red if it is going the other way. |
| Monthly audit | Is the goal still live? Does the metric still move? Kill the charts nobody reads. |
| Kill rule | If a metric has not changed after two reviews, stop the experiment or pick a new metric. |

## Angles

### 1. "You don't need a dashboard. You need one number."

Use on: landing page, data-driven post.

The toolkit's strongest claim is restraint. One sheet and one metric, with no BI tool and no second login. Founders who have not looked at their numbers tend to assume the fix is a big analytics project. The copy should take that weight off.

Sample lines:
- You don't need a dashboard yet. You need one number you write down every week.
- If you can't say which number you want to move, start there. That's an afternoon, and it's the most useful afternoon you'll spend this month.

### 2. "An hour to set up, fifteen minutes a week"

Use on: data-driven post, prioritization post, lead form helper text.

The toolkit lays the setup out as six small steps that add up to about an hour, then a fixed 15-minute review. The honest framing is that the setup is small and the discipline is the hard part.

Sample lines:
- The setup is about an hour in a spreadsheet. Keeping the weekly review is the hard bit.
- A fixed slot beats "we'll look at it when we have time", because that time never comes.

### 3. "Numbers and the story in the same row"

Use on: data-driven post.

The tracker keeps the one-line insight next to the number, so nobody has to build slides to explain a week.

Sample lines:
- Write the number, then one line on why it moved. Keep them in the same row so the reason doesn't get lost.

### 4. "Small tests, short fuse"

Use on: prioritization post, positioning post.

The guardrails keep every test short and cheap, and the kill rule stops zombie experiments. This is the bridge from data to prioritization: a priority is something you can test inside two weeks.

Sample lines:
- If you can't test it inside two weeks, it's too big. Cut it down until you can.
- When a number stops moving after two reviews, stop the experiment and pick a new one.

### 5. "Decide what 'enough' looks like before you look"

Use on: data-driven post, due diligence post.

The decision threshold is the cure for endless data gathering. It is also a useful thing to have ready before an investor meeting.

Sample lines:
- Before you run the test, write down what result would make you act.
- Know what would change your mind before somebody else asks.

### 6. "Every chart has to earn its place"

Use on: data-driven post.

Goal, then metric, then picture. A chart with no goal behind it gets deleted in the monthly audit.

Sample lines:
- No goal, no metric. No metric, no chart.
- Once a month, delete the charts nobody looked at.

### 7. "Landing pages are the cheapest experiment you have"

Use on: positioning post, data-driven post, landing page.

Several of the worked examples use a landing page per audience to test a message, with a form to count interest. This matches what Nathan suggested on the call.

Sample lines:
- One page per message, a form on each, and you can see what people came for.
- You find out which story lands by counting, not by arguing about it in a meeting.

### 8. "One owner per number"

Use on: prioritization post.

Shared accountability is no accountability. The toolkit assigns one owner to each metric, and the weekly review is each person explaining their own numbers.

Sample lines:
- Each number has one owner. In the review, that person says what worked and what didn't.
- When the lanes are clear, nobody has to chase anybody.

## Worked examples, anonymised

The toolkit applies a framework to six participant businesses. Use these as illustrations of range, without names or figures.

| Business type | The question they had | First metric the toolkit suggests |
|---|---|---|
| A manufacturer with a small marketing budget | Where does a limited budget do the most good? | Qualified leads per week |
| A consultancy that matches two groups of people | How do you explain the value to each side? | Matches per month |
| A health and supplements founder | How much evidence is enough before building? | New validated pain statements per week |
| A health-tech startup led by researchers | Which users do you go to first? | Demos booked per week |
| An education consultancy with a new product | What is the moment a new user gets it? | Active accounts per month |
| A startup advisor | How do you make go-to-market concrete for early founders? | Experiments closed per week |

Angle: the point of the table is that the first metric is small and countable in every case. None of them is revenue.

## Lines to avoid

- Anything that implies the toolkit guarantees an outcome.
- Any of the participants' names, budgets, percentages or targets.
- "Transform", "unlock", "supercharge" and other brochure words. The toolkit's own title page uses some of these, and the site should not.
- The course or institution the toolkit was first prepared for. That is a credential claim and is logged in `removed-claims.md`.
